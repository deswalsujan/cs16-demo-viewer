# Sujan's three refinements of 26 Nov 2026: what the reference code and the demos say

Sujan's brief of 26 Nov 2026: the Glock's sound, the legs while backing up and strafing, and the kill feed and the spectating screen in the game's look. Items 1 and 2 are built in 0.27.6 (`tests/logs/0.27.6.md`); item 3 waits for a mockup (IDEAS.md, **The kill feed and the spectating screen in the game's look**). Reference code: cs16-client (Velaron's, cloned 26 Nov 2026 at `4120cba`) and ReGameDLL_CS (`1f16a7f`). The checks in Sujan's game are in `~/Downloads/cs16-review-2026-11-26.zip` (a to d).

All demo moments are in SK vs Na`Vi, Train, DreamHack Winter 2011 (`sk-vs-navi-dhwinter2011-1111261019-de_train.dem`, protocol 48), with rounds and timers as the viewer's `roundTime` gives them.

## 1. The Glock's sound

- cs16-client, `cl_dll/events/event_glock18.cpp`: the sound is glock18-1 when `g_iWeaponFlags & WPNSTATE_GLOCK18_BURST_MODE` (or `g_bGlockBurstMode`), else glock18-2. `g_iWeaponFlags` is the local player's own weapon state (`cs_wpn/cs_weapons.cpp`, set from the predicted weapon; it returns before that line when he has no weapon). So the listener's own Glock picks the sound for everyone's Glock shots, and someone watching a demo gets glock18-2. Every other gun picks one of its sounds at random (`Com_RandomLong(0, 1)`); the viewer takes them in turn, which is left as it is.
- Each fire sound plays on the shooter's weapon channel (`PLAY_EVENT_SOUND`, `CHAN_WEAPON`, pitch 94 to 109), so a new shot cuts the one before; the viewer does the same (`playSound`'s channel key, `<slot>:w`).
- ReGameDLL, `wpn_glock18.cpp` and `weapons.cpp`: single shots are 0.15 s apart at the fastest (cycle 0.2 less 0.05). A burst's second and third shots come from `FireRemaining`, called from `ItemPostFrame` while `m_flGlock18Shoot != 0` with no time test (the FAMAS's line tests `gpGlobals->time > m_flFamasShoot`), so they fire on the next player frames, a few milliseconds apart; the 0.1 s in `FireRemaining` only paces the FAMAS. The burst's fire events carry nothing that marks them: `bparam2` is always false, and the punch angle (×100 on the first shot, ×10000 on the others) is 0 unless he was just hit.
- The demos agree: a burst arrives as three Glock events from one player in one frame (or two and one in neighbouring frames). Same-player Glock events in one frame, six test demos: SK vs Na`Vi, Train, DHW 2011 47 frames of three and 12 of two (393 shots); Na`Vi vs FX, Train, SEC 2011 4 and 5; mTw vs Lions, Nuke, DHS 2011 19 and 7; fnatic vs EG, Dust2, EM3 10 and 4; Sweden vs Norway, Dust2, ASUS ENC 2010 12 and 11; NoA vs Pentagram, Train 4 and 4. The events in one frame have different spread values (`fparam1`, `fparam2`), so they are separate shots; the few identical pairs are spread 0, 0 or 0.01 apart, at the 0.01 the demo stores. No event has a fire delay.
- A Glock-heavy round: R1 of SK vs Na`Vi, Train (68 Glock shots, 8 bursts): Na`Vi starix's single shots at 0:59 to 0:55, 0.3 to 0.4 s apart; his bursts at 0:37, 0:36, 0:34, 0:33, 0:29, 0:27 and 0:26, three events each, 0.5 to 0.6 s apart. No duplicates.
- Built in 0.27.6: glock18-2 for every Glock shot, the recorder's own in **POV mode** too. Open: whether his game plays glock18-1 for his own bursts when it plays a POV demo (IDEAS.md, **The Glock's sound for his own bursts in a POV demo**).

## 2. The legs

- cs16-client, `cl_dll/GameStudioModelRenderer.cpp`, `StudioEstimateGait` and `CalculateYawBlend`, with gait estimation always on (`m_fGaitEstimation = 1`, `StudioModelRenderer.cpp`): each frame the speed comes from the drawn origin's move since the last frame. Under 5 units/s he stands: the legs turn toward his view by the difference times dt × 4 (times dt for a frame of 0.25 s or more). Otherwise the legs point straight along the move (`atan2`); if his view is more than 120° from that, the legs turn 180° and the run plays in reverse. The upper body's turn is held to 90° either way (`blend_yaw`), the same mapping the viewer uses (`bs` in `posePlayer`).
- The viewer up to 0.27.5 (`prepAnim`): moving over 20 units/s, legs halfway toward the target at each sample, the flip at 90°; standing, 0.12 of the way toward the view each sample, about a third of the game's rate at ten samples a second. Moving sideways sits on the 90° line, so the run swapped direction and the legs spun.
- Moments in SK vs Na`Vi, Train, DHW 2011: Na`Vi ceh9, R1 1:14 to 1:11 (demo 9:32 to 9:35), moving at 90° to his view on average for 2.4 s and crossing the 90° line 9 times; Na`Vi edzie, R1 0:47 to 0:45, backing away at about 144° (both rules flip him; the control). Others: SK.face R20 1:42 to 1:40 (5 crossings), Na`Vi markeloff R19 1:27 to 1:26 (5). In this demo, 12,114 of the 248,448 samples where a player moved at all are between 5 and 20 units/s, which the game counts as moving and 0.27.5 counted as standing.
- Built in 0.27.6: the game's numbers, and between two samples the legs keep the angle of the move between them, so a flip is one step; the measurements are in `tests/logs/0.27.6.md`.

## 3. The kill feed and the spectating screen (not built; mockup first)

- cs16-client, `cl_dll/death.cpp`: up to 4 notices (`MAX_DEATHNOTICES`), each for `hud_deathnotice_time` (6 s, as the viewer). Rows 20 px apart from `YRES(32) + 2` for a player in his own view, and from `ScreenHeight / 5 + 2` for a spectator (`g_iUser1` set, as in every HLTV demo). Each row ends at the screen's right edge: the killer's name, 5 px, the weapon sprite, the headshot sprite, the victim's name, no other gaps. Names in team colours (`util.cpp`: T 1.0, 0.25, 0.25; CT 0.6, 0.8, 1.0; spectators and the world grey 0.8); the sprites drawn additive in 255, 80, 0, team kills 10, 240, 10. The viewer: up to 5 rows, each in a dark box with a border, 12 px from the top and right, T #e8574d, CT #5ea3e8, icons #ffa83c.
- Where the first row sits for a spectator: cs16-client says a fifth of the height; Sujan's YouTube POV screenshots of the 2011 build put it at about 12%, just under the top bar (Sujan, 26 Nov 2026). His own screenshots from the zip (d, R9 0:27) decide.
- The font: `DrawConsoleString`, the engine's console font. In Sujan's `valve/resource/TrackerScheme.res`, `EngineFont` is Verdana, weight 600, with a drop shadow: 13 px for 480 to 599 lines, 15 for 600 to 767, 16 for 768 to 1023, 21 for 1024 to 1299, 28 for 1300 to 1699, 35 for 1700 to 1899. That the console string uses `EngineFont` is the likely reading; his screenshots confirm it.
- The frame for the mockup: R9 0:27 of the same demo, **Player's eyes** on Na`Vi Zeus /A/: five kills since 0:33 (the last, Zeus's AK-47 on SK.f0rest), so the 4-row limit shows.
- What the viewer draws while following a player in an HLTV demo (**Player's eyes** and **Behind player**): the name box at the bottom middle (name in team colour, health, gun; a dark box with a border), and on his death the red wash and the "killed by" banner, then a living teammate after 2.5 s; the round pill at the top left and the header's score above the view; the kill feed at the top right; in **Player's eyes** the green crosshair in the viewer's three sizes, the kill marks and the sniper scope; labels over the other players (name, health, gun), **Damage numbers**, kill rings and glow (off with **Vanilla**); the camera bar and the "Press ? for help" line. It doesn't draw the game's spectator bars, which **POV mode** draws while he watches someone else (`drawSpecBars`, from `cstrike/resource/UI/Spectator.res`; `docs/pov-spectator-bars-mock.png`). The game's spectator panel is closed code, but its layout files (`Spectator.res`, `BottomSpectator.res`) are in Sujan's `cstrike/resource/UI`.
- Decided (Sujan, 26 Nov 2026): **POV mode**'s spectator bars also while following a player in HLTV demos; the mockup shows what they replace (the name box at the bottom, the header score) and what stays. His next message adds the POV radar dots, chat and radio lines, the flashbang behaviour and the scoreboard's spectator list to the same mockup.
