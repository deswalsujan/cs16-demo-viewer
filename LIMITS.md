# Known limits

What the viewer can't do yet, or does only roughly, in the current version (`VERSION`). Moved here word for word from the top of CHANGELOG.md on 30 Oct 2026 (Sujan's decision); every release updates it (AGENTS.md, "How each change ships"). Release entries in CHANGELOG.md still call this list "Known limits". Strict and LOOSE below are the wallbang finder's two kinds, shown in the viewer as **Wallbang** and **Partial wallbang** since 0.27.0 (STRICT WB and LOOSE WB up to 0.26.31).

- The 3D view runs 0.1 s behind the kill feed, timers, sounds and radar, as the game's demo player seems to draw players (0.24.0; matched on Dosia's kill on cArn, fnatic vs Moscow Five, Nuke, DreamHack Summer 2011, not confirmed in the 1.6 demo player's code). The wallbang finder and the picture check still judge at the killing shot. The damage numbers, the killing bullet's red skull (the ✕ before 0.26.16) and **Glow on hits** come with the kill feed since 0.26.3, beside the player as the 3D view draws him, and so does the health in the name bar and the 3D labels since 0.27.4 (FLEX0R (88) at his kill on R!Go-, Team Belgium vs France, Tuscan, ClanBase NationsCup XII, R21 0:39, as in Sujan's game). So at a kill row's moment the 3D view shows the victim 0.1 s before the shot: R!Go- in the open between the crates, behind the crate's edge at the shot itself; on the 83 test demos 56 of 534 strict wallbangs show 50 pixels or more of the victim there (`tests/logs/new-demos-feedback-2026-11-20.md`). Kill details' **Jump to this kill** and each bullet's jump pause 0.1 s later in 3D since 0.27.4, so the paused picture is the shot itself; played through, the 3D view stays 0.1 s behind.
- The game's demo player draws players about 0.1 s back, so at the kill feed a victim can show a sliver that the wallbang finder, judging at the shot, doesn't see: THREAT on LoordB (FX vs Lions, Nuke, DreamHack Winter 2010, R22 0:47) reads strict where Sujan sees his elbow (Sujan, 1 Nov 2026: the finder keeps judging at the shot). The same when the killer moves: walle on Neo (SK vs AGAiN, Train, Arbalet Cup Europe 2009, R18 1:06) reads strict, hidden behind a pillar at the shot, where 0.1 s back walle's eye was 5 units over and part of Neo shows. Since 0.26.22 a strict wallbang reads LOOSE when the killer's bullet on him 0.15 s or less before the kill did the full damage of a hit in the open (Edward-FoCuS on Gux, SK vs Na`Vi, Dust2, IEM4, R11 0:52); without such a bullet, as for THREAT on LoordB, it stays strict, and since 0.27.4 a hit the hit finder pinned on another shooter isn't such a bullet (shad on prophe7, BIH vs SLOVENIA, Nuke, 18 Jan 2009, R9 1:24: his teammate's hit in the open 0.09 s before). Sujan, 20 Nov 2026: the labels judge the moment of the shot only.
- Wallbang damage is worked out from the demo's health readings and hit sounds, since HLTV demos carry no "who hit whom" (0.24.0). The killing bullet shows only the health the victim had left. Each hit goes to the enemy who fired just before it with his crosshair closest; demos that lose fire events (most of 2008 and 2009) fall back on who was aiming at him, holding any gun since 0.27.4 (a pistol, SMG or shotgun hit is in the open, since its bullet stops at the first surface: WUZZ's USP on bAiL, WeMadeFox vs ATE Gaming, Dust2, MSI Beat IT! 2010, R20 1:14, went to a Deagle 15 units off through 899 units of map up to 0.27.3), and a hit with two enemies about as close is left out. In demos that keep fire events, a pistol, SMG or shotgun shot still doesn't compete for a hit, so such a hit can go to a rifle aiming near him or to no one: letting them compete there changed two of Sujan's confirmed answers (`tests/logs/new-demos-feedback-2026-11-20.md`). The finder doesn't cap how much cover a hit through cover passes. A bullet through another player loses damage like one through a wall, so a hit with a player on the line never makes a loose wallbang. A hit is judged on the crosshair line and the line to his middle (to his head for a headshot sound) and, since 0.26.22, when its damage is what the gun does in the open to a leg, the chest or the stomach at that distance, the line to that part, a leg on five heights from his feet to his hips since 0.26.24: it's in the open when any of them is clear. A hit with the flesh sound on a player without armour can do what a leg does in the open when it's a body hit through a wall, so with only part of his legs in view it can read as in the open (THREAT's two M4A1 hits at R2 of Na`Vi vs fnatic, Train, IEM4 final, in the By-eye round). A leg hit through a wall that happens to do a body hit's damage in the open could be missed, but no wall lets a bullet keep that much. A teamkill through a wall isn't listed in the Wallbangs tab (0.26.22); the Kills list tags every teamkill TK.
- Kill details and the direct hits in **Damage numbers** (0.26.16) use the same hit finder as wallbang damage: a hit is listed only when the demo's health readings, a hit sound and one enemy who fired (or, in demos that lose fire events, aimed) all line up, so a hit with two enemies about as close, a hit from a teammate, or a drop with no hit sound isn't listed, and a burst of bullets between two health readings can read as fewer, bigger hits. The killing bullet's number is the health the victim had left, not the bullet's damage. Where a bullet hit is the hit sound's head or body; chest or arm, stomach and leg are estimates from the damage, by the game's multipliers and armour, marked *, and "body" when more than one fits or the bullet went through a wall. "Earlier:" names a grenade or a fall only when the hit finder found one, and says "source unknown" otherwise. Only the live rounds have hit details, and only with the map loaded. In **POV mode** there are none, and since 0.26.30 no kill details box: the recorder's demo has only his teammates' health. A kill whose victim was never in a snapshot before he died, as on a skip in the recording, has no number for the killing bullet, since there's no spot to draw it at (0.26.17; three at R21 1:30 of EG vs eXecutors, Train, EM4 finals).
- An error while drawing no longer freezes the viewer (0.26.18): playback and the other parts of the view go on, but the part that failed (the 3D view, the radar, the kill feed, the HUD) can stay missing or out of date for as long as the error comes back, and only the first error in a page shows a toast. **Copy debug info** lists each different error once, with where it was thrown. A 3D map whose build fails says so in the 3D view and isn't tried again until another demo or map is opened; the 2D view still works.
- **Hide all overlays** (0.26.16, named **Vanilla** up to 0.26.31) turns the single switches off and brings them back when it's switched off; what has no switch of its own (kill markers and the ring, the death cam's red wash and banner, the C4 countdown) stays hidden while it's on. With it on, the round pill says "C4" after the plant, without the countdown. **Fixed light** (0.27.2) goes off with it and comes back after; the planted C4 and its blink stay, as the game's demo player shows them.
- A loose wallbang found from damage needs one of the killer's bullets through a surface 0.5 s or less before the kill (0.24.0; a millisecond more since 0.26.1, since the demo's times are rounded). A kill whose only bullet through cover came earlier stays a normal kill. Since 0.27.4 this also holds for a kill whose recorded aim at the shot is clear, with part of the victim in view and the line to his middle through cover, when the aim one snapshot before (0.16 s or less) went through cover with its end within 24 units of his middle (bartZi on Edward, Na`Vi vs WinFakt, Train, ESWC 2011, R8 1:29); one whose aim swung off the cover earlier than that stays a normal kill. Since 0.27.5 also for a kill whose aim, its recoil added, ends more than 24 units from the part the kill hit with the line to that part through cover (PeRRy on REAL, Norway vs Romania, Tuscan, 12 Feb 2009, R14 1:31).
- HLTV demos don't record the first-person weapon's animation, so it's rebuilt from shots, weapon switches and the player's body animation. Timing can differ slightly from in-game, and idle variations won't match. If a `v_` model is missing, no gun is shown.
- The 3D picture uses one fixed curve for everyone (0.26.31): since 0.27.2 gamma 3, brightness 2, texgamma 2, lightgamma 2.5 and no overbright, Sujan's own settings in the game (Sujan, 20 Nov 2026; Valve's defaults with `gl_overbright` 1 up to 0.27.1), as Xash3D FWGS builds them, whatever the player set in his own game; with other settings there, the game looks brighter or darker than the viewer. The map draws each face's first light style only, at its normal value, so a light the map switches or flickers isn't followed. The sky is the map's own pictures (0.27.2), through texgamma as Valve's engine draws them (Xash3D doesn't); a map whose pictures are missing gets desert's, as the game does, and without desert the grey-blue of up to 0.27.1.
- Players, their guns, bodies, shells and his gun in **Player's eyes** and **POV mode** are lit as the game lights them (0.26.31; Xash3D's R_EntityDynamicLight and R_StudioLighting): by the lightmap under them, from the direction it slopes away, or by the sky's colour from the server's sun direction when the line toward the sun reaches the sky, so in a dark corner they are hard to see, as in the game (Sujan, 12 Nov 2026). The light under a model is read from the world only, as the game reads it (Xash3D also reads doors and other brush entities, and the ceiling), from the face's first light style, as the map is drawn. The gun flash and other dynamic lights light nothing. **Team colours** keeps the even light every model had up to 0.26.30 (ambient 0.75 and a sun of 0.5 from above), so red and blue stay clear in the dark, and **Fixed light** (0.27.2, off by default) gives every model that light, skins and all, the map keeping the game's light.
- The gun flash, shells and blood puffs (0.26.27) are the game's own sprites and shell models, read from the picked Half-Life folder (`cstrike/sprites/muzzleflash1.spr` to 3, `valve/sprites/blood.spr` and `bloodspray.spr`, `valve/gfx/palette.lmp` for the blood's red, the shell models); a folder without them shows none. They follow the demo's fire events, so a shot whose fire event the demo lost (most of 2008 and 2009, and a weapon switch right after a shot) has no gun flash and no shell; since 0.27.5 an AWP or Scout shot comes from the shooter's body animation instead (`ref_shoot_rifle`), with its flash, shell, gunshot, rechamber and the scope's drop, which the game's demo player doesn't play (Sujan's check, 23 Nov 2026); other guns' lost shots still have none, since their shoot animation runs on through a spray, and the wallbang finder reads fire events only. The gun flash from behind sits where the game puts it, at the gun model's muzzle or, for the M4A1, the Scout and the M3, mid-gun at the hand, so from straight behind the viewer's **Behind player** camera can show only a sliver of it past his head, where the game's chase camera shows it (RobbaN at R5 C4 0:21, SK vs Na`Vi, Train, DreamHack Winter 2011, seen by Sujan in the game, 9 Nov 2026; IDEAS.md, **The Behind player camera against the game's chase camera**). It lights nothing: the game's short light on nearby models and walls isn't drawn. Shells show only in **Player's eyes** and **POV mode**, for the player whose eyes the view is, and bounce on the map's walls and floors as if each faced along an axis; doors and players don't stop them, as in the game. Blood shows only where the server sent a blood sprite: none on a body hit through armour, as in the game; a helmet headshot's sparks and the blood stream on a headshot without a helmet aren't drawn.
- Custom models are named in the load summary but not checked further yet. One with an unusual skeleton may pose oddly; compressed or 24-bit sounds stay silent. A sound that hasn't loaded when it's due isn't played; since 0.27.5 the gunshots load first when a demo opens, then the rest.
- A demo that's cut off, never finished or damaged partway plays up to where its readable frames end (0.16.0). Anything after a damaged stretch is left out, even if readable frames follow it. A file damaged in its first frames, before the match begins, still can't be played.
- Which rounds count is exact when the server's admin plugin announces the start ("Live !", "lo3", "3 restart and go !") and the end of each half in chat. Where it announces only the start (MYM's "LIVE!!!") and the file starts after the first half's announcement, the stretch before the first announced start counts as a half when it's a whole one (15 rounds, 12 in MR12) from 0:0 with the sides swapped after it (0.27.5; Sweden vs Poland, Nuke, 8 Apr 2009); a shorter one doesn't count. Plugins word these differently, and a wording the viewer hasn't met yet falls back to a guess from the restarts, which can still count a warmup or miss a round. A file holding only a warmup, opened on its own, can show one round. Inside the announced match, a restart that keeps the teams on the same sides before the half is over replays the half (0.26.19), so a restart after 12 to 14 rounds where the admins agreed to keep the score would read as a replay: the chat that says so ("4-0 score stays") isn't read.
- HLTV demos store about ten snapshots a second, so everything between two snapshots is an estimate. A flick that starts and ends between snapshots can't be recovered, and a sharp turn can look slightly rounder than it was. The view always passes exactly through every recorded snapshot.
- Players' legs follow cs16-client's rules (0.27.6): along the move from 5 units/s, turned around with the run reversed past 120° from the view, turning toward the view when standing; the game works this out each frame from the drawn position, the viewer once per snapshot, which gives the same legs while he moves in a straight line and can differ for a fraction of a second when he starts, stops or turns. Valve's own client is closed code, so cs16-client's reading of it is the reference; Sujan's check in his game is in the **By-eye round**.
- Every Glock shot plays glock18-2 (0.27.6), the sound the game plays for someone watching: the game picks glock18-1 only while the listener's own Glock is in burst mode. Whether his game plays glock18-1 for the recorder's own bursts in a POV demo isn't checked (IDEAS.md, **The Glock's sound for his own bursts in a POV demo**).
- The Glock's gun in **Player's eyes** shows the burst animation only where the demo proves a burst: three of the player's Glock rounds within 0.12 s (0.27.7). A burst the demo cut short (two events), or spread over more than 0.12 s (45 of the 1,460 three-shot groups in the test demos), shows the single-shot animation, as the game's demo player shows every Glock shot. In **POV mode** his own bursts are told by his clip losing 3 rounds within 0.12 s; no test demo has one of his own Glock bursts, so that path is checked on single shots only. A shot that empties the clip plays the ordinary shot, where the game plays the gun's empty animation (IDEAS.md, **The last shot of a clip in Player's eyes**).
- **POV mode**'s radar dots are 2 of the radar sprite's 128 units across (0.27.7), from Sujan's YouTube POV of a 2011 build (about 1/68); not checked in the 25th Anniversary build.
- The Tab scoreboard lists the spectators one per line (0.27.7), so a server with many of them makes the list longer; the board is cut at the bottom of the view if it doesn't fit.
- At the moment of a kill, the shooter's crosshair can sit slightly off the victim, usually under 1.5° (through an AWP's 1x zoom that's up to about 60 pixels on a laptop screen). This comes from the demo, not the viewer: see 0.10.0, "Discussed, not changed".
- The sniper scope is rebuilt from the zoom click sounds, since HLTV demos don't record zoom (see 0.10.0). A click the recorder didn't hear would put the zoom one step off until the player's next weapon switch, death or round. Clicks are timed to about a tenth of a second.
- Theatre mode switches on by itself only with the viewer's own full screen (F or the Full screen button). The browser's full screen (F11 on Windows, the green window button or Ctrl+Cmd+F on a Mac) doesn't tell the page, so press T there.
- "Quality: auto" lowers 3D sharpness at most once per visit and doesn't raise it again on its own. Pick "Quality: high" to go back.
- Demos recorded by a player (POV demos) play, with two limits: the player's game only receives the players near them, so others drop in and out of view, and the wallbang finder is less reliable there than in HLTV demos. A recording that switched maps plays the map it spent longest on. They play through his eyes in **POV mode** (0.25.0), with no setting since Sujan signed it off (11 Nov 2026, 0.26.30); a file without the record of his own view plays like an HLTV demo, with him at the map's zero point, since the server never puts his own position in the snapshots. Their teams are grouped from the sides people played on: two groups join only when their rounds on the same side outnumber their rounds apart (0.26.28), so on a public server, where players are moved between teams, each person goes with the team he played for most. A round starts at each restart the server announces (0.26.28). Unless the admin's plugin announced the match in words the viewer knows, every round played to a result counts from the first stretch between restarts with 4 or more real rounds (played to a result, 3 kills or more; 0.26.28, Sujan, 10 Nov 2026): the rounds before it were played before the match (the GP Tourney's two before its "Tourney War Config Has Been Loaded", which the viewer doesn't read; short rounds between a public server's start of map restarts). A match restarted after 3 rounds or fewer would lose those rounds, and a restart that replays one round mid-half (after a player dropped) is read as a restart, not a replayed half, since nothing was announced.
- **POV mode** (0.25.0) finds no wallbang damage, and so no loose wallbang from damage: the server sends a player only his teammates' health. His crosshair's colour and size come from `cstrike/config.cfg` in the picked folder; without it, the game's defaults (green, medium). The other players are drawn 0.1 s behind his own view, as in the HLTV viewer: Sujan's decision of 29 Oct 2026, since neither 0 nor 0.1 s lined up clearly better at his kills. His aim for the wallbang finder is the snapshots' aim, as for everyone else, not the exact aim his own frames hold. In its Tab scoreboard (0.26.28) his own HP is his HUD's, and on a server that sends no teammate's health at all (the GP Tourney's) there's no HP column; a player his game didn't receive at that moment shows alive or dead from the server's messages, with an empty gun cell and no C4 or kit tag.
- **POV mode**'s HUD effects, hit arcs, plant and defuse bar and Tab scoreboard (0.26.29) follow the messages his game got and cs16-client's rules, timed from the demo, so a paused or slowed demo shows them as they were at that moment. The hit arcs need `valve/sprites/640_pain.spr` in the picked folder, and the + and − over the money `plus` and `minus` in `cstrike/sprites/hud.txt`; without them, none. A spectator who starts watching a planter or defuser partway gets the bar at the percent the server's BarTime2 gives (AlliedModders' list of Half-Life 1 messages, ReGameDLL's `observer.cpp`): this case hasn't been seen in the game, and cs16-client draws it differently (Sujan, 11 Nov 2026). The Tab scoreboard he held is the viewer's own, by side, not his game's look. The ! on **See through walls**, the notices at the top middle and the hidden shortcuts line (**Help ?** in the bottom bar stays) are in **POV mode** only; the notices step down below the kill feed while one of its rows would reach them, so at 1280x720, with five long rows in the feed, the anti-wallhack note opened from the ! reaches the middle of the view until it's closed. Since 0.26.30 his HUD, crosshair and radar are added onto the picture, as the game draws them, so over a near-white wall they fade into it as in the game; the spectator bars and the plant and defuse bar's background are laid over a flashbang's whiteout at 60% black, as cs16-client draws them (FillRGBABlend, alpha 153: spectator_gui.cpp, DrawUtils::DrawRectangle), so at full white they show as grey bars, the spectator bars with their text; the plant bar's orange frame and fill are added onto the picture (FillRGBA, hud/timer.cpp), so over its grey they read light yellow (Sujan, 11 and 12 Nov 2026; for the spectator bars, from a POV video: Steam's own spectator panel is closed code, so that video and cs16-client are what's known of it). The crosshair is added only when config.cfg has it translucent (the game's default); his **Crosshair** row in **View settings** sets its size, the colour always from config.cfg.
- The kill feed and the spectator bars in the game's look (0.28.0) are the 3D view's; the 2D view keeps the viewer's own feed (Sujan, 28 Nov 2026), and the free camera has the feed without the bars. Their letters are the browser's own smoothed Verdana with a 0.75 smear (Sujan, 28 Nov 2026), so their edges are soft where the game's are hard, and through his JPEG they read a little darker than his game's text; they aren't checked on Windows Chrome yet. They're laid out in the game's 800x600 units and follow the view's height, not **Interface size**; at a scale that isn't whole each game pixel has at most one soft screen pixel at its edge. In Theatre mode the side panel, when it slides in, covers the feed and the bars' right part, and the header covers the top bar's score (the header then shows its own). The bars' score is the match's, as the header's, not the server's TeamScore, which restarts at a half's restart. "Spectators n" shows only when the HLTV demo carries the viewer count; in a POV demo the line holds his money. Without the player's `d_` icon for a gun in `cstrike/sprites/hud.txt`, the gun's name in orange takes its place; without `gfx/vgui/800_timer.tga` (cstrike's or Half-Life's), the clock has no icon.
- Chat and radio lines (0.28.1) are the 3D view's, as the kill feed. An HLTV demo carries only dead players' all-chat: no team chat, no living player's lines and no radio words (the radio's sounds only); a POV demo what the recorder's game got. Server and plugin lines and the game's name-change line aren't drawn (Sujan, 27 Nov 2026). The top line's capitals at 437 while spectating are from his 800x600 shot; the 430 while he plays and the 16 units between lines are from his YouTube POV of an older build, not yet checked in his game, and the lines fill from the top as in Valve's code. A sender the demo has no team for yet (just joined) is grey. A line with a place in it (ReGameDLL's location_area_info) uses ReGameDLL's own words, seen in no demo. The letters are the kill feed's (the browser's smoothed Verdana). While the bars are up the strip at the bottom left sits inside the bottom bar; in a window too small for it there even as icons (a view under about 400 px high with a long name), it sits above the bar, over the chat's 4th and 5th lines, never over the name.
- His radar in **POV mode** (0.26.30) is the solid one (cl_radartype 1) for everyone since 0.27.2 (Sujan, 19 Nov 2026; the see-through one up to 0.27.1), whatever config.cfg says, and shows his living teammates and the bomb, never enemies. A teammate his game didn't receive is where the last Radar message put him, which the server sends once a second and only while that teammate moved 64 units or more, so one standing still keeps his last spot. Without `cstrike/sprites/radaropaque640.spr` in the picked folder, no radar.
- The planted C4 (0.27.2) is the game's model where the demo has the bomb's entity, 0.1 s behind the kill feed like every player, and its blink the server's glow sprites, on the moment itself. A POV demo gets the bomb's entity and its blinks only while the bomb is in the recorder's PVS: with no entity, the model stays where the demo last had it in the round, or where it first has it later, since the bomb never moves; the orange marker, on the floor under BombDrop's spot, only for a bomb the demo never has in that round (7 of 84 plants in the POV test demos), and the blink only when the server sent it. Without `models/w_c4.mdl`, the orange marker; without `sprites/ledglow.spr`, no blink.
- Flashbangs (0.26.30) show only in POV demos, as **Flashbang dot** and **Flashbang whiteout**, both off by default: they follow the white screen fades his game got, which the server also sends while he watches a teammate in first person, and a fade the server cleared (when he stopped watching a blinded teammate, or respawned) ends there. HLTV demos have none, as in the game's demo player. Chat and radio lines show over the white, as the game's (0.28.1).
- The Half-Life folder is chosen on every visit, in every browser, with the browser's ordinary folder pick (0.26.11; Sujan, 2 Nov 2026). Chrome and Edge ask whether to "upload" the folder's files, counting every file in it: that's their word for letting the page read a folder, and nothing leaves the computer. The page sees the files as they were at the pick, since nothing tells it when the folder changes: a demo added or recorded while the page is open shows up only after **Choose again**, and one deleted or changed after the pick is found only when the page tries to read it, when it's clicked or when the player comes back to the page with the start screen showing; it's then taken off the list with a note. Up to 0.26.10 Chrome and Edge kept the folder between visits and picked up changes by themselves, except for Steam's default folder, which their folder access refuses.
- On maps with a lot of scenery (de_tuscan above all) the demo has no grenade in flight and no gun on the floor: the old engine sends at most 256 objects per snapshot and the map's own objects fill nearly all of them. Smokes, HE explosions and flashbang pops still show where and when they went off (see 0.14.0), but there's no flight path to draw.
- A map split over several demo files is joined by hand (**Join parts…** on the load card or in the Rounds tab, or since 0.27.3 two to five demos picked at once in **Open .dem from elsewhere**). More than 5 picked at once open nothing, and of several .dem files dropped on the page only the first opens. The viewer suggests the order from the recording times in the file names (HLTV's own, and since 0.27.4 the game's auto-record plugins' names on POV demos, "…_27-03-2025_15.10.23", "…_00-23-35_28-07-2022", "…_D-[08_24_2021]_T-[00_44]"), or from the rounds; with 3 or more parts and no times in the names it can't, so the parts are put in order with the arrows. A demo with no real Steam IDs (a LAN server where every player has "0") isn't offered as a part; it can still be joined by hand, with players matched by name. A later part that picks up on the same sides partway through a half carries the half on (0.26.19): what the earlier part counted still counts, unless the two together come to more than a half, when the later part replayed it. That needs the admin's announcements, so a break on a server that announces nothing is counted as before; and the viewer can't tell a server crash from a recording that stopped, so the Join card doesn't say why a recording broke. Two kinds of wrong order in the test maps pass the rounds' checks: the Forge parts of fnatic vs mousesports, IEM6 Guangzhou swapped, and the overtime files of mTw vs Na`Vi, Tuscan, ESWC 2010 among themselves; the card warns when an order differs from them, with **Use the recorded order**. Since 0.27.3 the card warns when a part starts from 0:0 after earlier parts played rounds that count (only a warning, **Join parts** stays on), except where it starts a new half or overtime from 0:0 (Sujan, 20 Nov 2026): at a half's end, changing sides only where the halves end, with no more than 6 rounds on one side after round 30. So a copy of an overtime file (stretches of 3 and 3) placed right after its original isn't warned: Tuscan's part 2 twice reads "The rounds agree." with no warning. A second half of an MR12 map restarted from 0:0 is warned, since its 12 rounds run across round 15 (the 7 joined test maps are all MR15) (`tests/logs/0.27.3.md`).
- Two Steam accounts are one person only when both connections used the same name, not a placeholder such as Player or (1)Player, during the live rounds (0.26.19). A player who changed account and name at once counts as two; a public server's IDs, which aren't Steam accounts, join people by name as before. Two connections on the server at the same time are never one person, whatever name or Steam ID they share (0.27.1; Sujan, 17 Nov 2026), so a player whose game crashed and who came back on a new slot before the server dropped the old one would count as two; no test demo has such a case.
- A round played between two recordings counts in the score from the game's scoreboard, but its kills are in no file, so player stats leave it out.
- Whether rounds after 16 count can't be read from the demo. Since 0.26.21 they count by themselves when the teams played on to round 30, with at least 4 rounds after the 16th win, each with a winner and 3 or more kills, as in IEM4's group stage; otherwise the match stops at 16. Fewer rounds after 16 can't be told from a server that played on after the match (iFNG FX vs fnatic, Dust2: one round after 16:13, confirmed 16:13), so a map that counted all 30 and ended 16:12 or closer (fnatic vs EG, Dust2, EM3 Global Finals 2009, 3 rounds after it) stops at 16 until **Count them** is switched on for it; **Stop at 16** turns the automatic count off for a demo.
- The match can't start before both teams are full (0.26.21): rounds before the first round of a file with 5 players on each side, 5 s into it, don't count. A match that really started with a team short, and never had both teams full before its first rounds, would lose those rounds; after both teams have been full, every round counts whatever the numbers.
- The team names come from the clan tag most of a team's players share, through the list of known teams (Sujan, 20 Nov 2026; 0.27.4): the whole tag, any part of it ("KOREA.eSTRO" reads eSTRO), or a known team's name of 4 letters or more it starts with ("fnaticMSI" reads fnatic, "mymTw.com" mTw); a tag not in the list reads as the players wrote it, capitals and all (TEAM BELGIUM, BURNING, K1CK). A tag loses a short bracketed bit at its end and never keeps an unpaired bracket ("SLOVENIA [o]" reads SLOVENIA), and is cut at 20 letters (12 up to 0.27.3). With no tag, the file name's team, then the admin's text (ESWC's AdminBot: "1st Warmup - SK(CT) vs Navi(T)", since 0.26.21); up to 0.27.3 those two came before the tag. A tag holding a web address (a part ending in .se, .com, .net and the like) isn't taken unless it's a known team's ("@mymTw.com" reads mTw); the file name's team takes its place ("dt.REASPEL.se" of dAT team, IOL Final4 2011, reads Dateam). Only ( ) [ ] { } are brackets, and the separators around a tag go with them ("-Eq-" reads Eq). - Latency in the Tab scoreboard (0.26.30) comes from the server's ping messages: an HLTV demo gets them every 2 s, a player only while he holds Tab, so in a POV demo it's what his last Tab hold showed, blank before his first. The scoreboard has the game's look for every demo type (0.26.30); its # column (the 1 to 0 keys) shows only in an HLTV demo with 10 players or fewer listed.
- The Tab scoreboard lists each player under the side he's on at that moment (0.26.28), as the game's scoreboard does, each side headed by the team with most of its people on it at that moment, with that team's score (the round's team only on a tie; Sujan, 11 Nov 2026); at a half's team swap, in the seconds before the next round starts, a player still on his old side shows under the other team's heading. A team's player the demo gives no side at that moment (no TeamInfo yet, a reconnect) stays under his team's side, dead or alive from the kill feed; Spectators are only people never on a team, and a slot the server gave a team but never a player isn't listed.
- Names, chat and the admin's text are read as UTF-8 when their bytes are valid UTF-8 (0.26.28), as the game sends them; a name the server cut partway through a letter keeps its whole letters and loses only the cut one ("D r A G ᶠ.." in Match 3's POV demo), and a string that isn't UTF-8 otherwise reads one character per byte, as before. - The demo search looks at file names and the date each row shows (0.27.4): the recording date from the name, which the viewer reads in HLTV's and the game's auto-record plugins' forms, or else the file's own date, which can be the day it was copied. A demo whose name says neither the teams, the map nor a date (`FX-vs-sk-iem5-inf.dem`) is found by its file's date or by scrolling. The list is in the order of the files' own dates, newest first, whatever date a row shows.
- The wallbang finder rules out a kill that needed more damage than any shot through a wall can do (0.15.5). It needs the killer's shots in the demo, except for the AWP and the Scout, which count one bullet when none is recorded (0.26.26: neither fires twice in 0.6 s), unless the server marked a hit on the victim (a blood sprite or a helmet's sparks) earlier in the 0.6 s before the kill; around a jump in the recording it can't tell, and the kill stays as the walls alone say. The rule trusts the victim's last health reading before the kill, which the server sends at every hit. Since 0.26.7 the wall is one surface on the killer's crosshair line, the one whose material (from `cstrike/sound/materials.txt` in the picked folder) lets the most through; the game multiplies every surface the bullet passes, but the recorded crosshair line can cross more surfaces than the bullet did, so a kill through two thin walls can still be listed. Without the materials list in the folder, every wall counts as wood (0.6), as before 0.26.7. Damage bursts take wood's 0.6 for every wall.
- The thickness a wallbang shows (the WB tag's tooltip, **Copy list**'s "(N units)") is measured on the crosshair line the demo records, which leaves out the recoil the bullet flew with, so late in a spray the line can run into the ground before the wall: walle on XEK (SK vs EYESports, Inferno, Arbalet Cup Europe 2009, R26 1:33) reads 903 units, where the bullet's line crosses 212 of one wall. Which kills are wallbangs is judged on the same recorded line. Both are parked together (Sujan, 9 Nov 2026), except that since 0.27.5 a clear recorded aim is checked again on the bullet's line, the killing shot's recoil from its fire event added, and that line is the aim when it goes through cover (RobbaN on whiMp, SK Gaming vs mTw, Tuscan, WCG 2008 final, R30 1:22): a kill whose fire event the demo lost, or a Galil or FAMAS kill (their recoil overflows the event's field), has no recoil to add, the spread isn't added (the event cuts it to 0.01, 0.57°), and a recorded aim through cover is never cleared by the recoil (doing that broke 33 of Sujan's confirmed answers).
- Health and weapon under the names in 3D show at any distance (0.16.0). With many players far away, the labels can overlap.

- Some HLTV recordings skip ahead: at a few moments every player jumps far between two snapshots while the demo's game clock runs on as if 0.1 s passed (FX vs Lions, Nuke, DreamHack Winter 2010: 7 times). What happened in between isn't in the file, so no viewer can show it. Since 0.19.0 the viewer marks each skip and tags a wallbang that lands on one UNSURE, since the finder can only judge it from where everyone stood after the skip. A skip is found from what it does to the game (players moving faster than anyone can, health lost with no hit, and since 0.26.25 a kill arriving in the snapshot where a new round's players appear at the spawns), so one where nobody moved or got hurt can't be found; checked against the bomb's fuse on 47 demos, none was missed (0.20.0).
- The 2D map fits above the controls along the bottom of the view (0.26.14) where it opens, after **Reset view** and when the window changes size; dragged or zoomed by the player, it can go under them. In Theatre mode it fills the view, since the controls come in only with the bottom bar.

- **Player's eyes** and **POV mode** show the game's 4:3 picture whole (0.26.15): 90 degrees across a 4:3 picture, with more room at the sides on a wider view, as the game does in 4:3 stretched, 4:3 with black bars and 16:9 with "Allow widescreen Field of View". Someone who plays 16:9 without that option sees less top to bottom in the game than in the viewer. A view narrower than 4:3 (a 5:4 monitor, a 1024x768 window, half of a 1920x1080 screen) keeps 90 degrees across and shows more top and bottom, so guns and players look smaller there than in the game (20% at 1280x1024). There's no 4:3 view option (black bars or stretched) yet.
- **Interface size** (0.26.13) can't reach what the browser draws itself: hover tooltips (the text that shows when the mouse rests on a button), the open list of a menu such as Quality, and scrollbars keep the system's size, so on a big screen they stay small. The crosshair, the sniper scope, **POV mode**'s HUD and the round announcements keep their own sizing (they follow the view, as the game's do), and the wallbang picture check always draws at 640x400, so strict and LOOSE never depend on the screen.
- The page's text uses fonts already on your computer (0.26.9): Tahoma, and Verdana for headings and the round announcements (Verdana is the game's font). - The wallbang finder's last check draws the killer's view at the shot (0.22.0), so it needs the 3D engine and the victim's player model.
