# Known limits

What the viewer can't do yet, or does only roughly, in the current version (`VERSION`). Moved here word for word from the top of CHANGELOG.md on 30 Oct 2026 (Sujan's decision); every release updates it (AGENTS.md, "How each change ships"). Release entries in CHANGELOG.md still call this list "Known limits".

- The 3D view runs 0.1 s behind the kill feed, timers, sounds and radar, as the game's demo player seems to draw players (0.24.0; matched on Dosia's kill on cArn, fnatic vs Moscow Five, Nuke, DreamHack Summer 2011, not confirmed in the 1.6 demo player's code). The wallbang finder and the picture check still judge at the killing shot. The damage numbers, the killing bullet's red skull (the ✕ before 0.26.16) and **Glow on hits** come with the kill feed since 0.26.3, beside the player as the 3D view draws him.
- The game's demo player draws players about 0.1 s back, so at the kill feed a victim can show a sliver that the wallbang finder, judging at the shot, doesn't see: THREAT on LoordB (FX vs Lions, Nuke, DreamHack Winter 2010, R22 0:47) reads strict where Sujan sees his elbow (Sujan, 1 Nov 2026: the finder keeps judging at the shot). The same when the killer moves: walle on Neo (SK vs AGAiN, Train, Arbalet Cup Europe 2009, R18 1:06) reads strict, hidden behind a pillar at the shot, where 0.1 s back walle's eye was 5 units over and part of Neo shows. Since 0.26.22 a strict wallbang reads LOOSE when the killer's bullet on him 0.15 s or less before the kill did the full damage of a hit in the open (Edward-FoCuS on Gux, SK vs Na`Vi, Dust2, IEM4, R11 0:52); without such a bullet, as for THREAT on LoordB, it stays strict.
- Wallbang damage is worked out from the demo's health readings and hit sounds, since HLTV demos carry no "who hit whom" (0.24.0). The killing bullet shows only the health the victim had left. Each hit goes to the enemy who fired just before it with his crosshair closest; demos that lose fire events (most of 2008 and 2009) fall back on who was aiming at him, and a hit with two enemies about as close is left out. A bullet through another player loses damage like one through a wall, so a hit with a player on the line never makes a loose wallbang. A hit is judged on the crosshair line and the line to his middle (to his head for a headshot sound) and, since 0.26.22, when its damage is what the gun does in the open to a leg, the chest or the stomach at that distance, the line to that part, a leg on five heights from his feet to his hips since 0.26.24: it's in the open when any of them is clear. A hit with the flesh sound on a player without armour can do what a leg does in the open when it's a body hit through a wall, so with only part of his legs in view it can read as in the open (THREAT's two M4A1 hits at R2 of Na`Vi vs fnatic, Train, IEM4 final, in the By-eye round). A leg hit through a wall that happens to do a body hit's damage in the open could be missed, but no wall lets a bullet keep that much. A teamkill through a wall isn't listed in the Wallbangs tab (0.26.22); the Kills list tags every teamkill TK.
- Kill details and the direct hits in **Damage numbers** (0.26.16) use the same hit finder as wallbang damage: a hit is listed only when the demo's health readings, a hit sound and one enemy who fired (or, in demos that lose fire events, aimed) all line up, so a hit with two enemies about as close, a hit from a teammate, or a drop with no hit sound isn't listed, and a burst of bullets between two health readings can read as fewer, bigger hits. The killing bullet's number is the health the victim had left, not the bullet's damage. Where a bullet hit is the hit sound's head or body; chest or arm, stomach and leg are estimates from the damage, by the game's multipliers and armour, marked *, and "body" when more than one fits or the bullet went through a wall. "Earlier:" names a grenade or a fall only when the hit finder found one, and says "source unknown" otherwise. Only the live rounds have hit details, and only with the map loaded. In **POV mode** there are none: the recorder's demo has only his teammates' health. A kill whose victim was never in a snapshot before he died, as on a skip in the recording, has no number for the killing bullet, since there's no spot to draw it at (0.26.17; three at R21 1:30 of EG vs eXecutors, Train, EM4 finals).
- An error while drawing no longer freezes the viewer (0.26.18): playback and the other parts of the view go on, but the part that failed (the 3D view, the radar, the kill feed, the HUD) can stay missing or out of date for as long as the error comes back, and only the first error in a page shows a toast. **Copy debug info** lists each different error once, with where it was thrown. A 3D map whose build fails says so in the 3D view and isn't tried again until another demo or map is opened; the 2D view still works.
- **Vanilla** (0.26.16) turns the single switches off and brings them back when it's switched off; what has no switch of its own (kill markers and the ring, the death cam's red wash and banner, the C4 countdown) stays hidden while it's on. With it on, the round pill says "C4" after the plant, without the countdown.
- A loose wallbang found from damage needs one of the killer's bullets through a surface 0.5 s or less before the kill (0.24.0; a millisecond more since 0.26.1, since the demo's times are rounded). A kill whose only bullet through cover came earlier stays a normal kill.
- HLTV demos don't record the first-person weapon's animation, so it's rebuilt from shots, weapon switches and the player's body animation. Timing can differ slightly from in-game, and idle variations won't match. If a `v_` model is missing, no gun is shown.
- Player models get even lighting, so they don't darken in shaded spots the way they do in-game.
- The gun flash, shells and blood puffs (0.26.27) are the game's own sprites and shell models, read from the picked Half-Life folder (`cstrike/sprites/muzzleflash1.spr` to 3, `valve/sprites/blood.spr` and `bloodspray.spr`, `valve/gfx/palette.lmp` for the blood's red, the shell models); a folder without them shows none. They follow the demo's fire events, so a shot whose fire event the demo lost (most of 2008 and 2009, and a weapon switch right after a shot) has no gun flash and no shell. The gun flash from behind sits where the game puts it, at the gun model's muzzle or, for the M4A1, the Scout and the M3, mid-gun at the hand, so from straight behind the viewer's **Behind player** camera can show only a sliver of it past his head, where the game's chase camera shows it (RobbaN at R5 C4 0:21, SK vs Na`Vi, Train, DreamHack Winter 2011, seen by Sujan in the game, 9 Nov 2026; IDEAS.md, **The Behind player camera against the game's chase camera**). It lights nothing: the game's short light on nearby models and walls isn't drawn. Shells show only in **Player's eyes** and **POV mode**, for the player whose eyes the view is, and bounce on the map's walls and floors as if each faced along an axis; doors and players don't stop them, as in the game. Blood shows only where the server sent a blood sprite: none on a body hit through armour, as in the game; a helmet headshot's sparks and the blood stream on a headshot without a helmet aren't drawn.
- Custom models are named in the load summary but not checked further yet. One with an unusual skeleton may pose oddly; compressed or 24-bit sounds stay silent.
- A demo that's cut off, never finished or damaged partway plays up to where its readable frames end (0.16.0). Anything after a damaged stretch is left out, even if readable frames follow it. A file damaged in its first frames, before the match begins, still can't be played.
- Which rounds count is exact when the server's admin plugin announces the start ("Live !", "lo3", "3 restart and go !") and the end of each half in chat. Plugins word these differently, and a wording the viewer hasn't met yet falls back to a guess from the restarts, which can still count a warmup or miss a round. A file holding only a warmup, opened on its own, can show one round. Inside the announced match, a restart that keeps the teams on the same sides before the half is over replays the half (0.26.19), so a restart after 12 to 14 rounds where the admins agreed to keep the score would read as a replay: the chat that says so ("4-0 score stays") isn't read.
- HLTV demos store about ten snapshots a second, so everything between two snapshots is an estimate. A flick that starts and ends between snapshots can't be recovered, and a sharp turn can look slightly rounder than it was. The view always passes exactly through every recorded snapshot.
- At the moment of a kill, the shooter's crosshair can sit slightly off the victim, usually under 1.5° (through an AWP's 1x zoom that's up to about 60 pixels on a laptop screen). This comes from the demo, not the viewer: see 0.10.0, "Discussed, not changed".
- The sniper scope is rebuilt from the zoom click sounds, since HLTV demos don't record zoom (see 0.10.0). A click the recorder didn't hear would put the zoom one step off until the player's next weapon switch, death or round. Clicks are timed to about a tenth of a second.
- Theatre mode switches on by itself only with the viewer's own full screen (F or the Full screen button). The browser's full screen (F11 on Windows, the green window button or Ctrl+Cmd+F on a Mac) doesn't tell the page, so press T there.
- "Quality: auto" lowers 3D sharpness at most once per visit and doesn't raise it again on its own. Pick "Quality: high" to go back.
- Demos recorded by a player (POV demos) play, with two limits: the player's game only receives the players near them, so others drop in and out of view, and the wallbang finder is less reliable there than in HLTV demos. A recording that switched maps plays the map it spent longest on. With **POV mode** off, the default until Sujan signs it off, they play like HLTV demos and the recording player stands at the map's zero point, since the server never puts his own position in the snapshots; with it on, they play through his eyes (0.25.0).
- **POV mode** (0.25.0) finds no wallbang damage, and so no loose wallbang from damage: the server sends a player only his teammates' health. His crosshair's colour and size come from `cstrike/config.cfg` in the picked folder; without it, the game's defaults (green, medium). The other players are drawn 0.1 s behind his own view, as in the HLTV viewer: Sujan's decision of 29 Oct 2026, since neither 0 nor 0.1 s lined up clearly better at his kills. His aim for the wallbang finder is the snapshots' aim, as for everyone else, not the exact aim his own frames hold.
- The Half-Life folder is chosen on every visit, in every browser, with the browser's ordinary folder pick (0.26.11; Sujan, 2 Nov 2026). Chrome and Edge ask whether to "upload" the folder's files, counting every file in it: that's their word for letting the page read a folder, and nothing leaves the computer. The page sees the files as they were at the pick, since nothing tells it when the folder changes: a demo added or recorded while the page is open shows up only after **Choose again**, and one deleted or changed after the pick is found only when the page tries to read it, when it's clicked or when the player comes back to the page with the start screen showing; it's then taken off the list with a note. Up to 0.26.10 Chrome and Edge kept the folder between visits and picked up changes by themselves, except for Steam's default folder, which their folder access refuses.
- On maps with a lot of scenery (de_tuscan above all) the demo has no grenade in flight and no gun on the floor: the old engine sends at most 256 objects per snapshot and the map's own objects fill nearly all of them. Smokes, HE explosions and flashbang pops still show where and when they went off (see 0.14.0), but there's no flight path to draw.
- A map split over several demo files is joined by hand (Join the parts, or Join with another demo… in the Rounds tab). The viewer suggests the order from the recording times in the file names, or from the rounds; with 3 or more parts and no times in the names it can't, so the parts are put in order with the arrows. A demo with no real Steam IDs (a LAN server where every player has "0") isn't offered as a part; it can still be joined by hand, with players matched by name. A later part that picks up on the same sides partway through a half carries the half on (0.26.19): what the earlier part counted still counts, unless the two together come to more than a half, when the later part replayed it. That needs the admin's announcements, so a break on a server that announces nothing is counted as before; and the viewer can't tell a server crash from a recording that stopped, so the Join card doesn't say why a recording broke. Two kinds of wrong order in the test maps pass the rounds' checks: the Forge parts of fnatic vs mousesports, IEM6 Guangzhou swapped, and the overtime files of mTw vs Na`Vi, Tuscan, ESWC 2010 among themselves; the card names the recording times when an order differs from them.
- Two Steam accounts are one person only when both connections used the same name, not a placeholder such as Player or (1)Player, during the live rounds (0.26.19). A player who changed account and name at once counts as two; a public server's IDs, which aren't Steam accounts, join people by name as before.
- A round played between two recordings counts in the score from the game's scoreboard, but its kills are in no file, so player stats leave it out.
- Whether rounds after 16 count can't be read from the demo. Since 0.26.21 they count by themselves when the teams played on to round 30, with at least 4 rounds after the 16th win, each with a winner and 3 or more kills, as in IEM4's group stage; otherwise the match stops at 16. Fewer rounds after 16 can't be told from a server that played on after the match (iFNG FX vs fnatic, Dust2: one round after 16:13, confirmed 16:13), so a map that counted all 30 and ended 16:12 or closer (fnatic vs EG, Dust2, EM3 Global Finals 2009, 3 rounds after it) stops at 16 until **Count them** is switched on for it; **Stop at 16** turns the automatic count off for a demo.
- The match can't start before both teams are full (0.26.21): rounds before the first round of a file with 5 players on each side, 5 s into it, don't count. A match that really started with a team short, and never had both teams full before its first rounds, would lose those rounds; after both teams have been full, every round counts whatever the numbers.
- The team names come from the file name (a team there that runs on past the clan tag all its players share reads the tag, since 0.26.25: "SK_vs_AGAiN_ArbaletEU" reads AGAiN; a known team keeps its full name), then the admin's text (ESWC's AdminBot: "1st Warmup - SK(CT) vs Navi(T)", since 0.26.21), then a clan tag most of a team's players share. A tag is cut at 12 letters ("Frag eXecuto"). When none of them names a team, the header says "Team 1" and "Team 2" (iFNG FX vs fnatic, `auto_ifng-...`). Click the name in the header to type it in; it's remembered for that demo. Left as it is for now (Sujan, 2 Oct 2026; parked in IDEAS.md).
- The demo search looks at file names only. A demo whose name doesn't say the teams or map (`auto_ifng-...`) is found by scrolling.
- The wallbang finder rules out a kill that needed more damage than any shot through a wall can do (0.15.5). It needs the killer's shots in the demo, except for the AWP and the Scout, which count one bullet when none is recorded (0.26.26: neither fires twice in 0.6 s), unless the server marked a hit on the victim (a blood sprite or a helmet's sparks) earlier in the 0.6 s before the kill; around a jump in the recording it can't tell, and the kill stays as the walls alone say. The rule trusts the victim's last health reading before the kill, which the server sends at every hit. Since 0.26.7 the wall is one surface on the killer's crosshair line, the one whose material (from `cstrike/sound/materials.txt` in the picked folder) lets the most through; the game multiplies every surface the bullet passes, but the recorded crosshair line can cross more surfaces than the bullet did, so a kill through two thin walls can still be listed. Without the materials list in the folder, every wall counts as wood (0.6), as before 0.26.7. Damage bursts take wood's 0.6 for every wall.
- The thickness a wallbang shows (the WB tag's tooltip, **Copy list**'s "(N units)") is measured on the crosshair line the demo records, which leaves out the recoil the bullet flew with, so late in a spray the line can run into the ground before the wall: walle on XEK (SK vs EYESports, Inferno, Arbalet Cup Europe 2009, R26 1:33) reads 903 units, where the bullet's line crosses 212 of one wall. Which kills are wallbangs is judged on the same recorded line. Both are parked together (Sujan, 9 Nov 2026).
- Health and weapon under the names in 3D show at any distance (0.16.0). With many players far away, the labels can overlap.
- A smoke cloud is drawn as one light green ball for as long as the smoke puffs (about 21 seconds, less when the round restarts). The game's own puffs drift and thin out unevenly, which the viewer doesn't copy.
- Some HLTV recordings skip ahead: at a few moments every player jumps far between two snapshots while the demo's game clock runs on as if 0.1 s passed (FX vs Lions, Nuke, DreamHack Winter 2010: 7 times). What happened in between isn't in the file, so no viewer can show it. Since 0.19.0 the viewer marks each skip and tags a wallbang that lands on one UNSURE, since the finder can only judge it from where everyone stood after the skip. A skip is found from what it does to the game (players moving faster than anyone can, health lost with no hit, and since 0.26.25 a kill arriving in the snapshot where a new round's players appear at the spawns), so one where nobody moved or got hurt can't be found; checked against the bomb's fuse on 47 demos, none was missed (0.20.0).
- The 2D map fits above the controls along the bottom of the view (0.26.14) where it opens, after **Reset view** and when the window changes size; dragged or zoomed by the player, it can go under them. In Theatre mode it fills the view, since the controls come in only with the bottom bar.
- The dropped bomb on the radar needs the bomb object in the demo, so on de_tuscan it's often missing (the 256-object limit above).
- **Player's eyes** and **POV mode** show the game's 4:3 picture whole (0.26.15): 90 degrees across a 4:3 picture, with more room at the sides on a wider view, as the game does in 4:3 stretched, 4:3 with black bars and 16:9 with "Allow widescreen Field of View". Someone who plays 16:9 without that option sees less top to bottom in the game than in the viewer. A view narrower than 4:3 (a 5:4 monitor, a 1024x768 window, half of a 1920x1080 screen) keeps 90 degrees across and shows more top and bottom, so guns and players look smaller there than in the game (20% at 1280x1024). There's no 4:3 view option (black bars or stretched) yet.
- **Interface size** (0.26.13) can't reach what the browser draws itself: hover tooltips (the text that shows when the mouse rests on a button), the open list of a menu such as Quality, and scrollbars keep the system's size, so on a big screen they stay small. A tooltip look of the viewer's own would scale with the rest; that's part of the open item **Tooltips, and feature names in bold in the viewer's text** (IDEAS.md, "User experience"). **Auto** goes by the window's size, so a small window on a big screen keeps 100%. The crosshair, the sniper scope, **POV mode**'s HUD and the round announcements keep their own sizing (they follow the view, as the game's do), and the wallbang picture check always draws at 640x400, so strict and LOOSE never depend on the screen.
- The page's text uses fonts already on your computer (0.26.9): Tahoma, and Verdana for headings and the round announcements (Verdana is the game's font). Where they aren't installed (most Linux machines), Liberation Sans, Arial or DejaVu Sans stand in, so the page looks a little different there.
- The round and C4 timers stand still while the server is paused (0.26.5), found where the server's own clock stands still while the demo's runs on. Playback itself still runs through a pause at the demo's pace: in AGAiN vs Na`Vi, Train, recorded 24 Oct 2011, R7, that is 40 s of everyone standing still. A pause shorter than half a second isn't counted.
- The C4 timer above the view counts down from a fixed 35 s, the fuse every tournament demo measured so far uses (0.21.0). A demo from a server with another fuse (the game's own default is 45 s) would read wrong; the time since the plant in the Kills and Wallbangs lists is right either way.
- The wallbang finder's last check draws the killer's view at the shot (0.22.0), so it needs the 3D engine and the victim's player model. Without them (no WebGL, a missing model) the finder's 15 points decide alone and every wallbang reads as strict. The Wallbangs list settles a few seconds after the map loads, while it says "Checking N from the shooter's eye…". The victim's pose at the shot is the viewer's estimate (HLTV doesn't record animation frames), so a sliver of a limb can be off by a few units. Strict and LOOSE are split at 50 pixels of the victim in view (0.23.0); no line fits every close call: NEO on ArcadioN (74) and gob b on Xizt (223) read LOOSE, where Sujan finds the victim hidden. A kill the finder's 15 points see, with the crosshair through cover, is drawn too and listed under 50 pixels: strict at 0, LOOSE from 1 (0.26.0). Fences, grates and invisible walls (render mode 4) are drawn with their holes open, as the game draws them (0.26.6): they stay cover, so a kill through one is a wallbang, LOOSE when the victim shows through the holes. 0 pixels isn't always hidden: REAL on alleN (Sweden vs Norway, Dust2, ASUS ENC 2010, R25 0:46) and Tentpole on zet (SK vs eSTRO, Dust2, WCG 2008, R1 1:35) read strict where Sujan saw part of the victim.
- A kill that lands on a skip across a round restart (HLTV left out the end of the round and the restart) is never listed as a wallbang: where everyone stood at the shot isn't in the file (0.22.0). Its kill details count the health that arrives with the skip in the round before it, with no source ("−27 at 0:14, source unknown"), and the killing bullet says the recording skips here (0.26.25).
